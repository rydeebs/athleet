Start with the campaign decision.

Choose what the partnership should help your business learn or achieve: introduce a product to a relevant local community, produce useful training content or test an offer with a specific audience. Then define work the athlete can actually deliver. Keep the business objective separate from a guarantee of sales.

Use sponsor discovery to assess athlete and audience fit. This page helps you plan the campaign after that initial selection: the scope, quote, permissions, reporting and approach to a season.

Audience and event

Name your target customer, market, race date and discipline. Explain why this athlete’s community fits. Distinguish existing audience evidence from estimated event attendance.

Offer and creative

Choose one message, a destination link if needed and an agreed call to action. Supply approved artwork and product information, with realistic review dates.

Success and proof

Agree how you will check the placement and content, who will provide metrics and when you will review the campaign. Keep targets distinct from contracted deliverables.

Choose a scope before asking for a price.

These are example package structures, not live offers or published rate cards. Every package requires an athlete quote and confirmation of event permissions. enduur does not publish verified average deal prices.

Compare proposals using the same scope. Ask what the athlete’s fee includes and whether printing, shipping, content production or photography are additional. Specify currency, fees and the final payable amount. The cost guide covers these pricing questions; the race value calculator supplies only an illustrative starting estimate.

Resolve rights and permissions early.

Confirm the placement against the current organizer rules for the event and division. The tattoo sponsorship guide explains the rule and artwork checks. A commercial agreement between an athlete and brand cannot override an organizer restriction.

Define whether the brand can repost images, edit them or use them in paid advertising. Agree channels, duration and territory where relevant. Obtain the necessary rights for third-party race photography before including it in the campaign.

Be precise about exclusivity. Name the product category and period rather than asking for a blanket restriction on all other sponsorships. An athlete’s category agreement does not create event-wide exclusivity.

Match the brief to the discipline

For HYROX, confirm the division and whether the proposal covers one athlete or a team. For triathlon, consider uniform rules and coverage across the three disciplines. For trail running, plan around packs, layers, access and weather.

Describe an approved fallback if the placement becomes unavailable. Agree who supplies artwork and prints, when the athlete approves them and what happens if materials arrive late.

For social content, make the sponsor relationship clear. The FTC’s Disclosures 101 guide (PDF) explains disclosure expectations for US-facing endorsements.

Build a report around evidence.

Delivery

Collect agreed placement photographs, post URLs and dates. Check those against the brief. An event image demonstrates the placement at that moment, not total spectator exposure.

Content performance

Record available post views, interactions and reporting periods. Avoid adding follower counts across platforms and describing them as unique campaign reach.

Business response

If you use links or codes, decide who reports clicks and redemptions. Report the measured results and attribution limits; do not promise a particular sales return.

Agree cancellation, injury, withdrawal and non-finish scenarios before the event. Separate completed preparation work from undelivered race placements. Funded bookings follow the payment and proof-review terms; confirm payment availability in the workspace before starting paid work.

Make a season concrete.

List every proposed race and the deliverables attached to it. Set deadlines, an allocation for each event and review points between races. An agreement for the first event does not automatically secure the athlete’s next season or establish a volume discount.

Your agreement checklist

  • Named athlete, races, dates and participation status
  • Approved placements, artwork and production responsibilities
  • Content formats, deadlines, proof and usage rights
  • Quoted price, currency, fees and payment conditions
  • Cancellation, substitution, partial delivery and reporting
